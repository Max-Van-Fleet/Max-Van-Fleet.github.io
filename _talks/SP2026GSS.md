This five-minute presentation was delivered as part of a joint graduate student seminar hosted by the Departments of Biostatistics and Statistics and Operations Research. I introduced NOAA’s current approach to constructing temporal distributions for design storms and then outlined a more data-driven framework based on hourly precipitation observations. The talk compared several ways of representing storm distributions—including density, cumulative distribution, and quantile functions—and explored the use of principal component and functional principal component methods to identify their dominant modes of variation.
